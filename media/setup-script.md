# How to set up: audio walkthrough script

This is the text of [setup-walkthrough.mp3](setup-walkthrough.mp3), read by a built-in Windows text-to-speech voice.
Each paragraph is one step. Pause the audio whenever you like.

Hi. This takes about ten minutes, and you can stop after any step. Nothing here costs money.

Step one. Open Google Drive on your computer or phone. Make a new folder and call it WORK-HQ, all capitals. This folder is the shared office for your helpers.

Step two. Inside WORK-HQ, make a folder for each app you want to try. Start with just one, for example a folder called claude, or chatgpt, or gemini. Inside that folder, make one more folder called out. That is where the app leaves its work.

Step three. Copy the template files from this kit into WORK-HQ. The most useful one to start with is TASKS. It is your to-do board. Also save a copy of the README in the folder and call it START-HERE, so every helper reads the same rules first.

Step four. Sign up for one free app. Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini are the easiest. Use the same Google account that owns your folder.

Step five. Connect the app to your folder. Open the app's settings and look for a part called Connectors, or Apps, or Extensions. Pick Google Drive and sign in. If it asks what to share, pick only the WORK-HQ folder. Why connect it? So the app can read your notes first, and save its work where you, and your other helpers, can find it. If the app cannot connect, that is fine. You copy its answer into its out folder yourself.

Step six. Paste the talk-short rule from the README into the app's custom instructions. Now it will answer you in a few plain lines.

Step seven. Paste the starter prompt for that app from the prompts folder. Then paste the interview prompt. Talk as long as you like about your work, what you enjoy, and what you would love to stop doing. The app turns your answers into a short task list.

That is it. Use that one app for a week. If it helps, add a second one and let it check the first one's work. You stay the boss. Keep what is good and skip the rest.
